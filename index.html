HOW CYBERCRIME APPEARS IN REAL LIFE

Recognise the schemes.

These are practical public-awareness scenarios and should not be confused with statutory offence titles.

BEC

Business Email Compromise

A criminal impersonates a trusted executive, supplier or contact to redirect a payment or obtain sensitive information.

SEXTORTION

Threats Using Private Content

A person threatens to expose intimate content unless the victim pays money, sends more content or complies with demands.

ROMANCE FRAUD

Trust Before the Demand

A false identity builds an emotional relationship and later requests money, gifts, cryptocurrency or financial assistance.

INVESTMENT / CRYPTO

Fake Returns & Withdrawal Fees

Victims are shown fake profits and pressured to send more money before supposed withdrawals can be released.

IMPERSONATION

Fake Accounts, Real Damage

Stolen names, images or branding are used to deceive contacts, obtain money or damage reputation.

ACCOUNT TAKEOVER

Your Account, Their Control

Passwords, OTPs or recovery methods are abused to take control of email, social-media, bank or other digital accounts.

TWO SIDES OF PREVENTION

Don't Fall Victim. Don't Perpetrate.

DON'T FALL VICTIM

  • Be suspicious of unusual urgency, secrecy or pressure.
  • Verify people and payment instructions through a second trusted channel.
  • Never disclose OTPs, PINs, passwords or recovery codes.
  • Question unsolicited investments, crypto demands and “guaranteed” returns.
  • Do not send money or intimate content because of threats or manipulation.

DON'T PERPETRATE

  • Do not impersonate people, institutions or businesses online.
  • Do not take over another person's account or use another person's payment details.
  • Do not threaten victims with private content or harass them online.
  • Do not help receive, move or conceal suspected fraud proceeds.
  • Online conduct can have serious real-world and legal consequences.
